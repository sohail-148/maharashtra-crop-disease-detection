1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & METHODOLOGY
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This study investigates whether post-hoc temperature scaling and validation-selected
confidence threshold rejection can reliably improve prediction safety and filter out
out-of-distribution (OOD) inputs (cross-crop leaves and non-leaf objects) without
compromising real-world operational utility.

Strict Methodological Safeguards:
  - Scalar temperature T was fit strictly on the validation split by minimizing NLL.
  - Rejection thresholds were selected strictly on validation split data.
  - Internal held-out test splits, Track B (external), Track C (real-world), and Track D
    (cross-crop & unrelated) served strictly as held-out evaluation sets.
  - All 4 production baseline models and Candidate D remain 100% untouched.

4. CORE EMPIRICAL FINDINGS & RESEARCH QUESTIONS
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A. Can confidence calibration improve probability reliability?
   - YES, on in-domain distributions. Temperature scaling successfully reduced ECE and NLL
     on both validation and held-out test splits across all four baseline models.
   - All four baselines exhibited T > 1.0 (ranging from 1.15 to 1.62), demonstrating that
     uncalibrated MobileNetV2 models systematically overstate their prediction confidence.

B. Does calibration meaningfully separate correct vs incorrect real-world predictions?
   - NO. Temperature scaling is a monotonic transformation (it preserves logit rankings).
     On external (Track B) and real-world (Track C) datasets, incorrect predictions continue
     to exhibit overlapping confidence distributions with correct predictions.
   - In Track B, errors have comparable or higher confidence than correct predictions.

C. Can a validation-selected threshold reject cross-crop and unrelated inputs?
   - NO. When applying validation-selected thresholds (such as 90% target accuracy):
     * An excessive fraction of wrong-crop leaves bypass rejection (25% - 75% false acceptance).
     * A substantial fraction of unrelated non-leaf images bypass rejection (30% - 70% false acceptance).
     * Simultaneously, 40% to 65% of legitimate real-world field predictions are discarded.

D. Is raw or calibrated softmax confidence alone sufficient for reliable OOD rejection?
   - NO. Post-hoc confidence calibration alone is fundamentally insufficient to safeguard
     against OOD, cross-crop, and non-leaf inputs in agricultural vision deployments.
   - The evidence strongly justifies proceeding to Phase 19B-2: Dedicated Two-Stage Gatekeeper
     Architecture (Crop Species Identifier & Leaf vs Non-Leaf Gatekeeper).
