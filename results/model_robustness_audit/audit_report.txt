EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
-----------------
Prior to full deployment and before making decisions regarding retraining, data augmentation,
or architecture changes, a comprehensive 4-track empirical robustness audit was conducted on
the 4 locked production models:
  - Tomato (tomato_baseline.keras, 10 classes)
  - Grape (grape_unified_baseline.keras, 7 classes)
  - Chilli (chilli_cold_baseline.keras, 5 classes)
  - Sugarcane (sugarcane_unified_baseline.keras, 11 classes)

EVALUATION METHODOLOGY:
  - TRACK A (Internal Held-Out Test Audit): 1,119 randomly sampled held-out test images
    (seed 42, up to 30/class) from our project splits.
  - TRACK B (Independent External Labeled Audit): 632 independently sourced, research-grade
    labeled images from open public datasets (Pakistan Tomato Field, PlantDoc, GLDD, AI Challenger,
    Ulfa Chili, Sabidarrow Sugarcane) with 100% verified ZERO hash collisions with project datasets.
  - TRACK C (Real-World / Internet User-Style Audit): 425 uncontrolled field/farmer photographs
    with complex backgrounds, varying daylight, weed clutter, shadows, and hands (369 HIGH quality).
  - TRACK D (Wrong-Crop & Unrelated Object Stress Test): 380 evaluations (300 cross-crop leaf tests,
    80 unrelated non-leaf benchmark images such as cars, persons, animals, furniture).

CORE HEADLINE FINDINGS:
1. TRACK A TO TRACK B COLLAPSE (LABORATORY OVERFITTING):
   - Tomato collapsed from 88.00% internal accuracy to 14.34% on independent external benchmarks.
   - Grape collapsed from 88.00% internal accuracy to 13.33% on independent external benchmarks.
   - Sugarcane dropped from 82.00% internal accuracy to 38.33% on independent external benchmarks.
   - Chilli maintained 56.25% internal vs 55.00% external (low internal accuracy, but zero lab drop).

2. REAL-WORLD FIELD TEST DEGRADATION (TRACK C):
   - Real-world field accuracy (HIGH quality): Tomato 30.92%, Grape 10.00%, Chilli 50.65%, Sugarcane 30.00%.
   - Overall real-world accuracy across all crops: 29.27% (108/369).

3. PATHOLOGICAL OUT-OF-DISTRIBUTION (OOD) OVERCONFIDENCE (TRACK D):
   - Models do NOT fail gracefully or express low confidence when fed completely wrong crops or non-leaf objects.
   - When fed unrelated non-leaf objects (cars, shoes, baboons, furniture):
     * Tomato model accepted them with 90.72% mean confidence (80% at conf >= 80%), predicting "Early Blight".
     * Grape model accepted them with 83.25% mean confidence (65% at conf >= 80%), predicting "Healthy Leaves".
     * Chilli model accepted them with 75.08% mean confidence (55% at conf >= 80%), predicting "Cercospora".
     * Sugarcane model accepted them with 71.13% mean confidence (35% at conf >= 80%), predicting "Mosaic Virus".
   - When fed cross-crop leaves (e.g. Tomato model fed Sugarcane leaves):
     * Tomato predicted "Yellow Leaf Curl Virus" with 94.53% mean confidence (80% of samples at >= 90% confidence, max 100.0%).

================================================================================
FAILURE MODE ANALYSIS & ROOT CAUSES
================================================================================

1. THE TOMATO "BLIGHT ATTRACTOR" (BACKGROUND SHORTCUT LEARNING):
   - Root Cause: The Tomato model was trained exclusively on PlantVillage, where leaves were photographed
     against uniform artificial laboratory backdrops (solid grey, black, or blue cards).
   - In contrast, real farmer and external images (Pakistan, PlantDoc, Sylhet) contain natural field backgrounds
     (soil, sky, shadows, weeds, hands).
   - The model learned the artificial background as a shortcut feature: when exposed to natural field foliage,
     it systematically defaults to "Early Blight" or "Late Blight" with 80% to 99.9% confidence, regardless of
     actual pathology. External healthy tomato leaves had a 0.0% accuracy, with 100% misclassified as Blight.

2. THE GRAPE "HEALTHY / MILDEW ATTRACTOR" (SENSOR ARTIFACT OVERFITTING):
   - Root Cause: Grape unified model trained on Niphad + 2024 datasets. The model overfitted to camera sensor
     profiles and lighting characteristics of those specific vineyard collections.
   - When presented with independent external images (baliwaka, GLDD), 70% of diseased leaves collapsed into
     "Healthy Leaves" or "Powdery Mildew" with high confidence.

3. THE CHILLI STABILITY PARADOX (DOMAIN ALIGNMENT):
   - Chilli was trained on the Krishna River Basin dataset, which was already an in-field farmer collection.
   - Because it was trained on real field conditions, it suffered virtually ZERO laboratory drop (56.25% Track A
     vs 55.00% Track B vs 57.73% Track C).
   - However, its baseline capacity is bottlenecked at ~55% due to class confusion between Cercospora,
     Nutritional Deficiency, and Healthy.

4. THE OOD HALLUCINATION VULNERABILITY:
   - Without an explicit out-of-distribution detection mechanism, background class, or confidence thresholding,
     the softmax layer forces every arbitrary input into one of the trained disease classes.
   - The extreme overconfidence (>90%) on completely unrelated objects (cars, baboons, sports balls) confirms
     that raw softmax confidence CANNOT be trusted as an indicator of real-world prediction validity.

================================================================================
ANSWERS TO THE 15 DECISION SUPPORT QUESTIONS
================================================================================

Q1: Does the Tomato model generalize to real-world / external tomato images?
ANSWER: NO. The Tomato model experiences a catastrophic generalization drop from 88.00% (internal)
to 14.34% (external) and 30.92% (real-world field). It is heavily overfitted to PlantVillage laboratory backdrops.

Q2: Which tomato classes are robust, and which are completely broken?
ANSWER:
  - Robust (Green): Late Blight (93.3% internal, 66.7% external, 80.0% real-world).
  - Moderate (Yellow): Early Blight (63.3% internal, 46.7% external, 50.0% real-world).
  - Broken (Red): Healthy (0.0% external), Leaf Mold (0.0% external), Target Spot (0.0% external),
    Mosaic Virus (0.0% external), Yellow Leaf Curl (3.3% external), Bacterial Spot (3.3% external),
    Septoria (10.0% external), Spider Mites (0.0% external). All collapse into Early/Late Blight.

Q3: Does the Grape unified model generalize to external grape images?
ANSWER: NO. Grape collapses from 88.00% internal accuracy to 13.33% external and 10.00% real-world field.
Only Healthy Leaves showed partial generalization (63.3%); Black Rot, Esca, Downy Mildew, Leaf Blight,
and Powdery Mildew all dropped below 10% on external field collections.

Q4: Does the Chilli model generalize, or is its 56% internal accuracy also degraded externally?
ANSWER: Chilli maintains its performance without domain collapse (56.25% internal vs 55.00% external vs
50.65% real-world). Because its training data came from real fields, it does not suffer from laboratory
backdrop overfitting; however, its overall accuracy is too low for standalone clinical diagnosis.

Q5: Does the Sugarcane model generalize to external sugarcane images?
ANSWER: PARTIALLY. Sugarcane drops from 82.00% internal to 38.33% external and 30.00% real-world field.
Red Rot generalizes moderately (50.0% external, 45.0% real-world), but Healthy Leaves dropped to 26.7%
external as field textures triggered false disease alarms.

Q6: What is the primary cause of generalization failure across models?
ANSWER: Shortcut learning on non-pathological background features (artificial solid-color backgrounds in
PlantVillage) and dataset-specific camera sensor noise, rather than invariant leaf lesion pathology.

Q7: What happens when models receive images of the WRONG crop?
ANSWER: Severe false alarms with extreme overconfidence. The models assign wrong-crop leaves to random
disease classes with up to 94.5% mean confidence, with 80% to 92% of predictions exceeding 80% confidence.

Q8: What happens when models receive completely UNRELATED non-leaf images?
ANSWER: The models confidently hallucinate diseases on ordinary objects (cars, animals, furniture). Tomato
accepted 80% of unrelated objects at >= 80% confidence (mean 90.72%), and Grape accepted 65% at >= 80% confidence.

Q9: Can raw softmax confidence be used as a reliability filter?
ANSWER: ABSOLUTELY NOT. The mean confidence on erroneous external predictions is 84.1% in Tomato, 76.9% in Grape,
and 61.0% in Sugarcane. Over 67% of Tomato errors have confidence >= 80%. High softmax confidence indicates
model overconfidence, not predictive accuracy.

Q10: Is Grad-CAM faithful and meaningful on external error cases?
ANSWER: YES. As demonstrated in Phase 15 and verified during the audit, Grad-CAM accurately exposes WHY the
models fail: on external and wrong-crop images, heatmaps consistently highlight the background soil, image borders,
or healthy vein structures rather than localized necrotic spots, confirming shortcut learning.

Q11: Which crops are viable for production deployment in their CURRENT state?
ANSWER: NONE are viable for unconstrained standalone deployment. However, with strict UI disclaimers and human-
in-the-loop validation:
  - Sugarcane (S1/S2): Conditionally viable for Red Rot triage.
  - Tomato: Conditionally viable ONLY for Late Blight / Early Blight differentiation.
  - Chilli and Grape: NOT viable for production diagnosis without retraining.

Q12: Is retraining necessary, or can post-processing / thresholding fix the issues?
ANSWER: RETRAINING IS MANDATORY. Post-processing (confidence thresholding) cannot fix the problem because the
models are 90%+ confident on their errors. The models must be retrained with natural field data, heavy background
augmentation, and out-of-distribution regularization.

Q13: What specific data interventions are required for each crop?
ANSWER:
  - Tomato: Must replace/augment PlantVillage with real field datasets (Pakistan field collection, PlantDoc,
    augmented outdoor backgrounds) to break the solid-background shortcut.
  - Grape: Must perform heavy color jitter, contrast normalization, and cross-vineyard domain generalization.
  - Chilli: Needs fine-grained lesion contrast enhancement and expert re-labeling of nutritional deficiency.
  - Sugarcane: Needs more diverse field stalk and canopy images across Indian growing regions.

Q14: Should a generic "Leaf / Non-Leaf" or "Crop Identifier" gatekeeper model be added?
ANSWER: YES, CRITICAL. A lightweight two-stage front-end gatekeeper is essential:
  Stage 1: Plant Leaf vs Non-Leaf Gatekeeper (rejects arbitrary objects).
  Stage 2: Crop Classifier Gatekeeper (verifies Tomato vs Grape vs Chilli vs Sugarcane before disease diagnosis).

Q15: What should the immediate priority be for the project roadmap?
ANSWER: Retraining the core models using cross-dataset field collections and implementing a Crop/Leaf Gatekeeper,
while maintaining Grad-CAM as an explanatory inspection tool.

================================================================================
PER-CROP RECOMMENDATIONS & ACTION PLAN
================================================================================

RECOMMENDATION A — TOMATO:
  - Action: MAJOR RETRAINING REQUIRED (Highest Priority).
  - Remedy: Integrate the Pakistan field collection (1,300+ raw field photos) and PlantDoc tomato images
    into the training corpus. Apply CutMix, Mosaic, and synthetic background replacement so the model learns
    lesion contours instead of background cards.

RECOMMENDATION B — GRAPE:
  - Action: RETRAINING WITH DOMAIN ADAPTATION.
  - Remedy: Combine Niphad and Grape 2024 with AI Challenger and GLDD vineyard data. Apply heavy photometric
    augmentations (hue/saturation/brightness jitter) and train with domain-adversarial or MixUp loss.

RECOMMENDATION C — CHILLI:
  - Action: CLASS REBALANCING & LESION-FOCUSED TRAINING.
  - Remedy: Re-examine nutritional deficiency vs cercospora labels. Incorporate higher-resolution localized
    lesion crops and test modern lightweight vision transformer backbones (MobileViT or EfficientNet-V2).

RECOMMENDATION D — SUGARCANE:
  - Action: FIELD DATASET EXPANSION & MULTI-LABEL MODELING.
  - Remedy: Broaden external field representations for Smut, Wilt, and Rust. Implement temperature scaling
    calibration to soften overconfident predictions.

RECOMMENDATION E — SYSTEM-WIDE ARCHITECTURE & FLASK INTEGRATION:
  - Action: IMPLEMENT TWO-STAGE GATEKEEPER & CALIBRATION.
  - Remedy:
    1. Implement Crop Gatekeeper: Verify crop identity before invoking the disease classifier.
    2. Implement Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Detector: Reject non-leaf or low-confidence inputs gracefully.
    3. Temperature Scaling: Calibrate logits so probabilities reflect true empirical likelihood.
    4. Maintain Grad-CAM: Keep Grad-CAM enabled in Flask UI to provide explainability for farmer trust.
