1. RESEARCH PROBLEM & MOTIVATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Deep learning vision models trained on laboratory-curated agricultural datasets
(e.g., PlantVillage, lab-mounted leaves) routinely report classification accuracies
exceeding 90%–99%. However, their operational utility in developing agricultural
regions like Maharashtra remains severely constrained when deployed on mobile devices
in unconstrained farm environments.

This investigation addressed the central research question:
"How effectively can a lightweight MobileNetV2 transfer-learning framework classify
diseases across multiple Maharashtra-relevant crop datasets, and how robustly do
these models generalize across independent external benchmarks, real-world field
photography, and out-of-distribution inputs?"

Four economically critical crops in Maharashtra were targeted:
  1. Tomato (10 classes | 14,529 images)
  2. Grape (7 classes | 6,203 images across Niphad and Mendeley datasets)
  3. Chilli (5 classes | 1,932 images from COLD 2024 raw subset)
  4. Sugarcane (11 classes | 8,926 images across Maharashtra and Large Sugarcane datasets)
Total Scope: 33 canonical disease classes across 31,590 verified images.

2. SYSTEMATIC ERROR ANALYSIS ACROSS THE FOUR CROPS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Using existing error-analysis artifacts from Phases 9, 10, 16, 18, and 19B-6:

A. Tomato Error Dynamics:
   - In-Domain Performance: 90.23% accuracy, 90.18% Weighted F1, 87.80% Macro F1.
   - Major Laboratory Confusion Pairs: Early Blight vs. Late Blight (continuous lesion progression);
     Spider Mites vs. Target Spot (stippling vs. small necrotic flecks).
   - Real-World Attractor Sink Collapse: Under field conditions (Phase 18, n=460), overall accuracy
     collapsed to 18.91%. Crucially, 87.6% (403/460) of all predictions collapsed into just two
     classes: Late Blight (217) or Early Blight (186).
   - False Positive Pathology: Healthy field leaves suffered a 100% false-positive rate (0/58 correct;
     32 predicted Late Blight, 23 Early Blight), as models interpreted background soil textures
     and natural solar glare as necrotic blight.
   - Persistent Weaknesses: Classes with minimal field support (Spider Mites: 2 field images, 0% acc;
     Target Spot: 0 Track C images, 0% acc; Leaf Mold: 0% acc).
    - Response to Field Adaptation (Phase 19B-6): Adding 71 authentic FieldPlant images reduced
      the concentration of predictions in previously dominant attractor classes in some evaluated
      cases and improved sensitivity to selected field phenotypes, surging Yellow Leaf Curl Virus
      recall from 5.36% to 42.86% (+37.50 pp) and Healthy recall from 0.00% to 27.59% (+27.59 pp),
      while Early Blight sink recall redistributed from 65.52% to 29.31%.

B. Grape Error Dynamics:
   - In-Domain Performance: 89.04% accuracy, 88.68% Weighted F1, 86.31% Macro F1.
   - Major Laboratory Confusion Pairs: Esca vs. Leaf Blight (marginal chlorotic/necrotic banding);
     Black Rot vs. Leaf Blight (early necrotic spotting).
   - Regional Dataset Gap: Niphad G1 laboratory photos achieved 95.35% accuracy, whereas Mendeley G2
     photos dropped to 84.12% (an 11.23 pp partition gap, p < 0.001).
   - Real-World Attractor Sink Collapse: In Phase 18 field testing (n=280), accuracy collapsed to 11.43%.
     74.3% (208/280) of predictions defaulted to Healthy Leaves (131) or Powdery Mildew (77).
   - Persistent Weaknesses: Bacterial Leaf Spot has zero field examples in any public benchmark (lab-only);
     Black Rot field accuracy was 1.7%–3.4%; Esca field accuracy was 0.0%–2.0%.
   - Response to Field Adaptation (Phase 19B-6): Adding 176 authentic HERMOS/ESCA field images
     surged Downy Mildew recall from 4.00% to 26.00% (+22.00 pp) and improved Healthy Leaves F1 from
     30.12% to 42.31% (+12.19 pp), raising Phase 18 field accuracy from 11.43% to 13.21%.

C. Chilli Error Dynamics:
   - In-Domain Performance: 63.79% accuracy, 62.36% Weighted F1, 57.75% Macro F1.
   - Major Laboratory Confusion Pairs: Nutritional Deficiency vs. Cercospora Leaf Spot; Murda Complex
     (leaf curl) vs. Healthy.
   - Root Cause: Subtle chlorosis and curling without distinct geometric margins created high
     intra-class variance and low inter-class separability.
   - Field Adaptation Success (Candidate D): Incorporating 800 authentic field images from Ulfa (2023)
     raised field accuracy from 50.60% (Baseline) to 71.08% (Candidate D) on Phase 18, and to 92.50%
     on Track B External, proving that authentic multi-source field data is essential for complex foliage.

D. Sugarcane Error Dynamics:
   - In-Domain Performance: 83.43% accuracy, 83.29% Weighted F1, 82.11% Macro F1.
   - Major Confusion Pairs: Mosaic vs. Healthy (faint striping vs normal leaf blade); Red Rot vs. Brown Spot.
   - Field Resilience: On the Phase 18 field benchmark (n=100), Sugarcane achieved 35.00% accuracy
     (and 38.33% on Track B external), outperforming Tomato (18.91%) and Grape (11.43%) baselines,
     partially driven by distinctive elongated linear morphology and strong contrast of red midrib lesions.

E. Diagnostic Role of Grad-CAM:
   - Explainability audit across 10 prioritized failure and success cases revealed that correct predictions
     correlate with high gradient concentrations centered on localized necrotic centers or pustules.
   - Misclassifications consistently exhibited dispersed gradients on peripheral leaf edges, soil
     backgrounds, or high-contrast shadows.
   - Scientific Qualification: Grad-CAM visual heatmaps indicate regions of high gradient magnitude
     and feature salience, but DO NOT establish causality. They demonstrate visual attention alignment,
     not proof of underlying causal reasoning or background shortcut mechanisms.

3. THE COMPLETE ROBUSTNESS PROGRESSION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The project executed a methodical, evidence-driven robustness campaign:

1. Baseline Establishment (Phases 1–8):
   - Proved that frozen ImageNet MobileNetV2 features achieve 83%–90% accuracy on controlled lab data.
   - Verdict: SUCCESSFUL IN-DOMAIN.

2. 4-Track Robustness Audit (Phase 16):
   - Audited models across Track A (Lab), Track B (External Research), Track C (Real-World), Track D (OOD).
   - Discovered the Generalization Gap: a 50–75 pp drop under unconstrained real-world conditions.
   - Discovered Attractor Sink Collapse and pathological softmax overconfidence (>85% on OOD).
   - Verdict: SCIENTIFIC BREAKTHROUGH / BASELINE LIMITATION IDENTIFIED.

3. Post-Hoc Softmax Calibration (Phase 19B-1):
   - Tested Temperature Scaling to reject OOD inputs using validation-selected confidence thresholds.
   - Finding: ECE improved significantly in-domain, but 60%–73% of wrong-crop leaves and non-leaf
     distractors falsely passed through. Softmax confidence cannot detect semantic OOD shifts.
   - Verdict: FAILED / CLOSED AS AN OOD GATEKEEPER.

4. Stage 2 Crop Species Identifier (Phase 19B-2 / 19B-2A):
   - Trained a 4-class species identifier on lab data (99.87% internal accuracy).
   - Evaluated on field data: accuracy collapsed to ~39% (Tomato recall dropped to 12.2%).
   - The observed field errors were consistent with sensitivity to domain-specific visual characteristics,
     causing it to reject legitimate field leaves.
   - Verdict: FAILED ON FIELD DATA / CLOSED FROM PRODUCTION GATING.

5. Stage 1 Binary Leaf vs. Non-Leaf Gatekeeper (Phase 19B-3 / 19B-4):
   - Curated a balanced 1:1 dataset (803 Leaf positives, 803 non-leaf negatives from 4 approved sources).
   - Results: 99.19% test accuracy, 100% Track D non-leaf distractor rejection, 96.55% field leaf retention.
   - Demonstrated that binary foliage features show strong retention across the evaluated field-leaf benchmarks.
   - Verdict: SUCCESSFUL VALIDATED RESEARCH COMPONENT.

6. Field-Data Acquisition & Taxonomy Correction (Phase 19B-5):
   - Screened candidate sources against 31,805 unique benchmark hashes (0 collisions).
   - Corrected taxonomy: disproved FieldPlant coverage of all 10 tomato classes; identified GVLiD as G2.
   - Acquired finite clean slices: 71 Tomato (FieldPlant) and 176 Grape (HERMOS/ESCA).
   - Verdict: SUCCESSFUL RIGOROUS SOURCING.

7. Field-Data-Assisted Retraining Experiment (Phase 19B-6):
   - Retrained Tomato and Grape on Kaggle GPU preserving exact baseline architecture and seed (42).
   - Evaluated multi-track deltas:
       Tomato Internal: 90.23% -> 89.72% (-0.50 pp) | Phase 18 Field: 18.91% -> 20.22% (+1.30 pp, MF1 +5.25 pp)
       Grape Internal:  89.04% -> 87.86% (-1.18 pp) | Phase 18 Field: 11.43% -> 13.21% (+1.79 pp, MF1 +2.00 pp)
   - Reduced the concentration of predictions in previously dominant attractor classes in some evaluated
     cases and improved sensitivity to selected field phenotypes (TYLCV recall: +37.50 pp, Downy Mildew recall: +22.00 pp),
     while causing modest reductions in internal test performance.
   - Verdict: SUCCESSFUL TARGETED ADAPTATION / CONTROLLED EMPIRICAL DEMONSTRATION.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. SCIENTIFIC INTERPRETATION & DEFENSE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Adding the selected independently collected field images was associated with modest overall
  field-benchmark gains and larger class-specific changes, while internal test performance decreased modestly.
- The intervention did NOT eliminate the broad real-world generalization gap (field accuracy remains ~13%–20%
  vs ~88%–90% internal test accuracy).
- We do NOT claim universal causality.
- We do NOT claim that field adaptation solved domain shift.
- We do NOT claim universal field generalization.
- We do NOT justify replacing the production models on the basis of these results alone.
- Production models in models/ remain locked and unchanged; experimental models remain offline research assets.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4B. FORMAL RESEARCH HYPOTHESES & EMPIRICAL VERDICTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
H1: Baseline Representational Capacity
    Hypothesis: MobileNetV2 with frozen ImageNet features is sufficient to achieve >80%
    classification accuracy across Maharashtra-relevant crops under lab conditions.
    Verdict: PARTIALLY SUPPORTED.
    Reason: The >80% condition was achieved for 3 of 4 crops: Tomato (90.23%),
    Grape (89.04%), and Sugarcane (83.43%), but not for Chilli (63.79% on single-source COLD 2024
    lab data due to fine-grained symptomatic overlap).

H2: Domain Shift & Generalization Gap
    Hypothesis: Models trained primarily on laboratory-curated leaf images suffer a statistically
    significant performance collapse when evaluated on unconstrained real-world field photography.
    Verdict: SUPPORTED.
    Reason: A 50 to 75 percentage point Generalization Gap was observed across all crops
    (Tomato: 90.23% -> 18.91%; Grape: 89.04% -> 11.43%; Chilli: 63.79% -> 50.60%; Sugarcane: 83.43% -> 35.00%),
    with predictions concentrating in majority attractor classes.

H3: Post-Hoc Confidence Calibration for OOD Gating
    Hypothesis: Post-hoc softmax temperature scaling on validation data will calibrate confidence
    estimates sufficiently to filter out-of-distribution non-leaf and wrong-crop inputs.
    Verdict: REFUTED.
    Reason: While temperature scaling reduced in-domain ECE by >50%, 60%–73% of wrong-crop
    leaves and non-leaf distractors bypassed validation-selected confidence thresholds. Softmax confidence
    operates on closed-set relative logit margins and cannot detect semantic OOD shifts.

H4: Decoupled Stage 1 Foliage Gatekeeper
    Hypothesis: Decoupling foliage verification into a dedicated binary classifier (Leaf vs. Non-Leaf)
    trained on diverse non-leaf negatives creates an effective pre-filtering safeguard.
    Verdict: SUPPORTED.
    Reason: The binary gatekeeper achieved 99.19% internal test accuracy, rejected 100.00%
    of Track D non-leaf distractors, and showed strong retention across the evaluated field-leaf benchmarks
    (96.55% field leaf retention across 2,229 uncurated field images).

H5: In-Situ Field-Data Retraining & Adaptation
    Hypothesis: Incorporating genuine in-situ field imagery into training data improves real-world
    field classification without degrading in-domain performance.
    Verdict: PARTIALLY SUPPORTED / MIXED EVIDENCE.
    Reason: The field-improvement component was observed on evaluated benchmarks (Tomato Phase 18
    accuracy increased from 18.91% to 20.22% with macro F1 +5.25 pp; Grape Phase 18 accuracy increased
    from 11.43% to 13.21% with macro F1 +2.00 pp; target class recalls surged: TYLCV recall from 5.36%
    to 42.86%, Downy Mildew recall from 4.00% to 26.00%), but internal test accuracy declined modestly
    (Tomato: 90.23% -> 89.72%, -0.50 pp; Grape: 89.04% -> 87.86%, -1.18 pp). Therefore, the field-improvement
    component was observed, while the no-in-domain-degradation condition was not fully satisfied.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. EXPERIMENTAL LIMITATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Finite Addition Volume: Tomato received only 71 field images; Grape received 176 field images.
2. Incomplete Class Coverage: Tomato additions covered only 3/10 classes; Grape covered 4/7 classes.
3. Persistent Unaugmented Classes: Several classes (Spider Mites, Target Spot, Bacterial Spot) remain
   completely unaugmented with near-zero field representation.
4. Geographic & Acquisition Disparity: Added field imagery originated from Cameroon, Turkey, and Italy,
   introducing distinct regional backgrounds differing from Maharashtra agricultural fields.
5. Setup Specificity: The experiment tests this specific transfer-learning setup and does not claim
   universal robustness across all agricultural contexts.
6. Finite Evaluation Benchmarks: Field benchmarks remain finite samples (n=460 Tomato, n=280 Grape)
   and may not capture all future agricultural variations.
7. Heterogeneous Response: Field augmentation does not improve every class uniformly (e.g. Esca dropped
   from 2.0% to 0.0%, Early Blight recall dropped due to sink redistribution).
8. Sample Size Dependency: Small class-level changes must be interpreted in conjunction with sample counts.
9. Asymmetric In-Domain Trade-Off: Adding uncurated field imagery slightly degrades in-domain lab accuracy
   (-0.50 pp on Tomato, -1.18 pp on Grape).
