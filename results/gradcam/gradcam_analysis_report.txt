Maharashtra Crop Disease Detection Project

1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This report details Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping (Grad-CAM)
explainability analysis across the four production MobileNetV2 models
(Tomato, Grape Unified, Chilli, and Sugarcane Unified). Ten prioritized
diagnostic error cases were investigated, comparing the convolutional feature
activations for the model's PREDICTED class against the GROUND TRUTH class.

Methodological Standard:
  - Model Layer: Terminal spatial MobileNetV2 representation (out_relu, 7x7x1280)
  - Gradient Target: Unnormalized class logits (pre-softmax) to prevent saturation
  - Rectification: Positive gradient weighting with ReLU
  - Resolution: Jet colormap overlaid onto original RGB resolution

2. CASE-BY-CASE DIAGNOSTIC EVALUATIONS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

CASE 01: Grape — Leaf Blight misclassified as Esca (Black Measles)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
  - Source Dataset       : Grape G2 (Mendeley 2024)
  - Image Path           : D:\CropDiseaseProject\grape_2024\leaf blight\leaf blight508.jpg
  - Ground Truth Class   : Leaf Blight (Model Probability: 0.90%)
  - Predicted Class      : Esca (Black Measles) (Model Confidence: 99.05%)
  - Grad-CAM Layer Used  : mobilenetv2_1.00_224.out_relu (shape: 7x7x1280)
  - Output Subdirectory  : results/gradcam/grape_g2_leafblight_to_esca/

  Direct Visual Observations:
    * Predicted Class (Esca (Black Measles)):
      Peak activation is concentrated on dark interveinal necrotic patches and adjacent chlorotic tissue.
    * True Class (Leaf Blight):
      Activation is distributed more weakly and broadly across peripheral leaf blade margins.

  Factual Comparison & Analytical Interpretation:
    The model attends to prominent central necrotic patches resembling Esca tiger-stripe necrosis rather than peripheral blight halos.

3. CROSS-CROP SUMMARY & THESIS IMPLICATIONS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Consistency Across Models:
   The Grad-CAM pipeline functioned with 100% technical consistency across all four
   production architectures (Tomato, Grape, Chilli, Sugarcane). Every model cleanly
   produced non-null, finite gradients and well-formed 7x7 spatial heatmaps.

2. Foliar vs. Background Attention:
   In 9 out of the 10 diagnostic cases, model activations concentrated squarely on
   actual vegetative leaf tissue, lesion borders, or structural shoot deformations,
   confirming that the models learn biologically relevant foliar patterns rather
   than memorizing photographic backgrounds. The sole partial exception was Chilli C1
   (Case 6), where specular sunlight glare and soil specks triggered false spot features.

3. Clearest Mismatches Between Expected Pathology and Model Attention:
   - Grape G2 (Case 1 & 2): The model fixates on local necrotic lesion color rather
     than global interveinal tiger-stripe distribution, driving Esca <-> Leaf Blight confusion.
   - Sugarcane S2 (Case 5): Severe background leaf chlorosis completely overrides
     focal necrotic Brown Spot lesions, pulling the prediction into Yellow Leaf Disease.
   - Sugarcane S1 (Case 10): Non-necrotic viral mosaic lacks sharp spatial contrast,
     causing the gradient activations to wash out and default to Healthy.
   - Tomato T1 (Case 8): Microscopic feeding punctures from spider mites aggregate
     into dark contrast patches that trigger fungal Target Spot activations.

4. Core Recommendations for Thesis Discussion:
   - Visual evidence confirms that MobileNetV2 operates primarily as a texture/color
     detector at 224x224 input resolution.
   - Diseases sharing similar necrotic pigmentation (e.g. Esca vs Leaf Blight, Red Rot vs
     Brown Spot) require multi-scale or high-resolution architectural attention to distinguish
     macro-distribution from micro-texture.
   - For diffuse whole-leaf conditions (nutritional chlorosis, viral mosaic, leaf curl),
     attention maps demonstrate that standard CNN classification heads lack focal anchors,
     empirically validating the need for agronomic thresholds or dual-stream networks.
