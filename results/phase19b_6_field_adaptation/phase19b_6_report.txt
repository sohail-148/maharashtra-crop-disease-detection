Key Dynamic:
In the baseline models, unrepresented or out-of-domain samples collapsed heavily into dominant attractor sinks
(Early Blight / Late Blight in Tomato; Healthy Leaves / Powdery Mildew in Grape). The addition of authentic field data
provided alternative probability basins, reducing overprediction of attractor sinks and restoring legitimate discrimination
for newly represented visual features, while preserving unaffected classes like Septoria and Bacterial Spot.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. SCIENTIFIC INTERPRETATION & DEFENSE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Adding the selected independently collected field images was associated with modest overall
  field-benchmark gains and larger class-specific changes, while internal test performance decreased modestly.
- The field-data intervention produced modest overall improvements on the evaluated external/field benchmarks
  (+1.30 pp on Tomato Phase 18, +1.79 pp on Grape Phase 18; +4.29 to +5.25 pp Macro F1).
- Several targeted classes showed substantially larger recall changes (Tomato Yellow Leaf Curl Virus +37.50 pp,
  Tomato Healthy +27.59 pp, Grape Downy Mildew +22.00 pp).
- Internal performance declined modestly for both adapted models (-0.50 pp for Tomato, -1.18 pp for Grape).
- The intervention did NOT eliminate the large real-world generalization gap (field accuracy remains ~13-20%
  compared to ~88-90% internal test accuracy).
- The results demonstrate an observed effect of adding the selected independent field data under this experimental setup.
- We do NOT claim universal causality.
- We do NOT claim that field adaptation solved domain shift.
- We do NOT claim universal field generalization.
- We do NOT justify replacing the production models on the basis of these results alone.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. EXPERIMENTAL LIMITATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Finite Addition Volume: Tomato received only 71 additional field images; Grape received 176 images.
2. Incomplete Class Coverage: The Tomato additions covered only Healthy, Mosaic Virus, and Yellow Leaf Curl Virus.
   Grape additions covered Powdery Mildew, Downy Mildew, Healthy Leaves, and Esca.
3. Persistent Unaugmented Classes: Several canonical classes remained completely unaugmented (e.g., Tomato Spider Mites,
   Target Spot, Leaf Mold, Bacterial Spot; Grape Bacterial Leaf Spot, Black Rot).
4. Geographic & Acquisition Disparity: Added field imagery originated from Cameroon (FieldPlant), Turkey (HERMOS),
   and Italy (Standalone ESCA), which introduce distinct regional visual characteristics differing from Maharashtra fields.
5. Setup Specificity: The experiment tests this specific field-data intervention and does not establish universal improvement.
6. Finite Evaluation Benchmarks: The external field benchmarks remain finite samples (n=460 Tomato, n=280 Grape) and may
   not represent all future agricultural field conditions.
7. Heterogeneous Class-Level Response: The experiment demonstrates that field-data augmentation does not improve every
   disease class uniformly (e.g. Esca recall dropped from 2.0% to 0.0%, Early Blight recall dropped due to sink redistribution).
8. Sample Size Dependency: Small class-level changes must be interpreted together with corresponding sample counts.
9. Asymmetric In-Domain Trade-Off: Adding external out-of-domain imagery slightly reduces in-domain feature alignment
   on lab-curated imagery (-0.50% to -1.18% test accuracy).
