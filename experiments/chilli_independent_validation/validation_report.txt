1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
================================================================================
Chilli Experiment 5 was conducted to resolve a pivotal research question emerging
from Experiment 4: Does Candidate D's superior generalization to field imagery
replicate when evaluated against an entirely new, unseen, independent field-data
source, or was its high performance on Track B (92.50%) and Track C (81.44%) an
artifact of the specific datasets utilized?

To answer this question under strict zero-leakage conditions, an independent
field evaluation dataset was curated from the PlantDoc dataset (Singh et al.,
CoDS-COMAD 2020) consisting of 115 unconstrained field images of bell pepper /
chilli leaves (Capsicum annuum).

Evaluation was executed purely in inference mode across three models:
  1. Production Baseline (models/chilli_cold/chilli_cold_baseline.keras)
  2. Candidate B (experiments/chilli_improvement/candidate_b/model.keras)
  3. Candidate D (experiments/chilli_field_experiment/candidate_d/model.keras)

KEY FINDINGS:
  - Candidate D achieved the highest performance across all primary metrics:
      * Validation Accuracy : 45.22% (+1.74% vs Baseline, +5.22% vs Cand B)
      * Weighted F1 Score   : 56.14% (+10.12% vs Baseline, +6.39% vs Cand B)
      * 2-Class Macro F1    : 56.26% (+11.07% vs Baseline, +6.53% vs Cand B)
      * 5-Class Macro F1    : 22.50% (+4.43% vs Baseline, +2.61% vs Cand B)
  - Healthy Leaf Discrimination: Candidate D achieved a dramatic improvement on
    healthy leaves in natural field settings (F1 = 57.78%, +23.21% over Baseline,
    +8.32% over Candidate B), with Healthy Precision reaching 70.27% (vs 50.00%
    for Baseline and 57.50% for Candidate B).
  - Suppression of False Alarms: While Baseline suffered massive false positive
    leaf spot errors on healthy leaves (58.49% of healthy leaves misdiagnosed as
    Cercospora), and Candidate B misdiagnosed healthy field leaves as Nutritional
    Deficiency (37.74%), Candidate D significantly reduced both failure modes.
  - Conclusion: Candidate D's field robustness is corroborated across three
    independent field benchmarks (Track B, Track C, and Experiment 5). However,
    overall accuracy on unconstrained wild field imagery remains moderate (45.22%),
    reflecting real-world challenges such as background clutter and false triggers
    on unrepresented classes.

4. EVALUATION METHODOLOGY & CONSTRAINTS
================================================================================
Operational Protocol:
  - Evaluation was executed locally in pure inference mode.
  - Zero retraining, fine-tuning, adaptation, or weight updates were applied.
  - Input Preprocessing: Standard MobileNetV2 pipeline:
      * Image resolution: 224 x 224 pixels (bilinear interpolation)
      * Normalization: tensorflow.keras.applications.mobilenet_v2.preprocess_input
      * Pixel value range: [-1.0, 1.0]
  - Evaluation Batch Size: 32
  - Metric Definitions:
      * Accuracy: Overall fraction of exact top-1 correct predictions over n=115.
      * Weighted Precision/Recall/F1: Ground-truth class support weighted metrics.
      * Represented 2-Class Macro F1: Arithmetic mean of F1 for Cercospora and Healthy.
      * All 5-Class Macro F1: Arithmetic mean across all 5 canonical classes
        (where unrepresented classes have support=0, F1=0.0).

6. MODEL-BY-MODEL DEEP DIVE
================================================================================
A. BASELINE MODEL (Frozen MobileNetV2, Trained on COLD):
  - Accuracy: 43.48% | Weighted F1: 46.02% | Represented Macro F1: 45.19%
  - Diagnostics:
    The baseline model exhibits severe bias toward predicting Cercospora Leaf Spot
    whenever complex natural backgrounds or outdoor leaf discolorations appear.
    Out of 53 truly healthy field leaves, the baseline misdiagnosed 31 as Cercospora
    (58.49% false positive rate on healthy leaves). Healthy recall was catastrophic
    at 26.42%, yielding a Healthy F1 score of only 34.57%. Because the baseline was
    trained on COLD images without field augmentation, it has learned to associate
    any natural leaf texture variation with fungal lesions.

B. CANDIDATE B (Fine-Tuned Stage 2 + Augmentation, COLD-Only):
  - Accuracy: 40.00% | Weighted F1: 49.75% | Represented Macro F1: 49.73%
  - Diagnostics:
    Candidate B substantially corrected the baseline's false Cercospora bias on
    healthy leaves (false Cercospora predictions dropped from 31 down to 7).
    However, Candidate B suffered an alternative severe failure mode: it
    over-predicted Nutritional Deficiency. Out of 53 healthy field leaves, Candidate
    B classified 20 (37.74%) as Nutritional Deficiency. On true Cercospora leaves,
    Candidate B classified 14 (22.58%) as Nutritional Deficiency. Because Candidate
    B learned subtle color variations during fine-tuning on uniform COLD backgrounds,
    natural sunlight gradients and outdoor chlorosis in PlantDoc leaves caused it
    to frequently trigger the Nutritional Deficiency output neuron.

C. CANDIDATE D (Multi-Source Field-Trained, COLD + 800 Curated Field Images):
  - Accuracy: 45.22% | Weighted F1: 56.14% | Represented Macro F1: 56.26%
  - Diagnostics:
    Candidate D achieved the most balanced and accurate feature representation:
      1. Healthy Foliage Recognition: Correctly identified 26 healthy leaves
         (Recall: 49.06%, Precision: 70.27%, F1: 57.78%). This represents a +23.21%
         F1 gain over Baseline and +8.32% F1 gain over Candidate B.
      2. Cercospora Precision: Reached 78.79% precision on Cercospora Leaf Spot
         (higher than Candidate B's 76.67% and Baseline's 53.73%).
      3. Suppression of False Nutritional Deficiency: False predictions of
         Nutritional Deficiency dropped from 34 total under Candidate B down to 17
         under Candidate D (a 50% reduction in this error mode).
      4. Murda Complex Activations: Candidate D predicted Murda Complex on 26 samples
         (16 Cercospora, 10 Healthy). In outdoor field photography, natural leaf
         curling, insect curling, and mechanical folds closely mimic the morphological
         curl symptoms characteristic of Murda Complex (thrips/mites). Candidate D's
         exposure to field data sensitized it to curl features, resulting in some
         over-triggering on curled non-murda field leaves.

9. ERROR MODE & DISTRIBUTION SHIFT ANALYSIS
================================================================================
1. Why All Models Score Below 50% Accuracy on Raw PlantDoc:
   The PlantDoc dataset differs profoundly from standard agricultural benchmarks:
     a. Extreme Background Clutter: PlantDoc images frequently contain soil,
        weeds, agricultural netting, drip irrigation pipes, hands, and overlapping
        plants of different species.
     b. Optical Variation: Strong sunlight, deep shadows, lens flare, and varying
        focal depths create severe domain shift compared to clean leaves.
     c. Multi-Pest Co-occurrence: Field plants often suffer from simultaneous
        leaf feeding (holes/tearing), physical bruising, and soil splashing.

2. Divergence in Error Signatures:
     - Baseline fails primarily through false positive lesion detection: It cannot
       distinguish natural leaf edge shadows or soil speckles from fungal spots.
     - Candidate B fails through chromatic confusion: Its aggressive fine-tuning on
       laboratory leaves made it sensitive to color shifts, causing outdoor yellowing
       and specular reflections to be misclassified as nutritional deficiency.
     - Candidate D fails through morphological confusion: Because Candidate D was
       trained on real field leaves depicting leaf curl and thrips damage, it tends
       to interpret natural 3D leaf curvature and curled margins as Murda Complex.

================================================================================
12. CAUSALITY DISCLAIMERS & LIMITATIONS
================================================================================
In accordance with scientific rigor, the following limitations must be noted:
  1. Partial Class Representation: Only Cercospora and Healthy leaves exist in this
     PlantDoc subset. Performance on Murda Complex, Nutritional Deficiency, and
     Powdery Mildew cannot be inferred from this experiment alone.
  2. Botanical Variation: PlantDoc samples include bell pepper (Capsicum annuum grossum),
     which shares species taxonomy with hot peppers (Capsicum annuum) but features
     broader leaf surface area.
  3. Unconstrained Field Noise: The absolute accuracy range of 40-45% indicates that
     no model tested is fully production-ready for unsegmented, uncropped wild field
     photos without an upstream leaf detector or bounding-box cropping stage.
  4. Non-Causal Generalization: While Candidate D's multi-source field exposure
     correlates strongly with improved field robustness, we cannot attribute 100%
     of the gain solely to data quantity versus diversity of background textures.
